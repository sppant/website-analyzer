Internal links are links from one page on your site to another. They do three jobs: they help search engines discover pages, they pass ranking signals ("link equity") between pages, and they give users a path through your content. Internal linking is one of the highest-leverage SEO tasks because you control it completely — no outreach required.

## How search engines use internal links

-   **Discovery** — a page with no internal links pointing to it is hard for crawlers to find, even if it is in the sitemap.
-   **Importance** — pages that receive many internal links are treated as more important. Your most valuable pages should be the most linked.
-   **Context** — the anchor text and surrounding content tell search engines what the linked page is about.

## Anchor text guidelines

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Use descriptive phrases: "our technical SEO checklist" | "click here", "read more", "this page" |
| Vary the wording naturally across links | Use the exact same keyword-stuffed anchor every time |
| Keep it concise — a few words | Link a whole sentence or paragraph |
| Link where it genuinely helps the reader | Force a link into every paragraph |

Empty or generic anchor text is a missed opportunity; heavily repeated exact- match anchors look manipulative. Aim for natural, varied, descriptive phrasing.

## Practical structure

1.  **Identify your key pages** — the ones you most want to rank (product pages, pillar guides, the homepage).
2.  **Link to them from relevant content** — every article that touches a related topic should link to the key page with descriptive anchor text.
3.  **Link between related articles** — this keeps readers on the site and builds topic clusters search engines can recognise.
4.  **Keep important pages within a few clicks of the homepage** — deep pages that take five clicks to reach get crawled less often.

## Common mistakes

-   **Orphan pages** — published, in the sitemap, but not linked from anywhere.
-   **HTTP internal links** on an HTTPS site — every click triggers a redirect.
-   **Self-links** — a page linking to its own URL adds noise, not value.
-   **Only linking from the navigation** — sitewide nav links matter less than contextual in-content links.
-   **Nofollowing internal links** to "sculpt" flow — this just wastes the link.

## A quick internal-link audit

For a given page, ask:

-   How many internal links point **to** it, and from where?
-   Do those links use **descriptive anchor text**?
-   Does the page link **out** to other relevant pages?
-   Are any of those links **broken**, HTTP, or pointing at redirects?
