The title tag is the `<title>` element in your page's `<head>`. It shows up in three places: the **search result headline** — the blue, clickable link — the **browser tab**, and the **default text when the page is shared or bookmarked**.

Unlike the [meta description](/blog/meta-description-guide), the title tag **is a ranking factor** — a modest but real one — and it is the on-page element you have the most direct control over. It is worth getting right.

## Ideal length

-   **About 50–60 characters** is the safe zone. Google truncates by pixel width — roughly **600px on desktop** — so a title made of narrow characters can run a little longer, and one with wide characters or many capitals gets cut sooner.
-   Longer titles still "work" — Google just shows an ellipsis — but the part you cared about may be the part that is hidden.
-   Put the **primary topic first** and the brand name last. If the important words sit at character 55, they may never be seen.
-   Preview it at real width with our [SERP preview tool](/serp-preview).

## Formats that work

| Page type | Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guide or article | `Topic: qualifier — Brand` | `Title Tag SEO: Length and Format — WebXDevelop` |
| Product | `Product, key attribute — Brand` | `Merino Base Layer, Men's — Alpkit` |
| Category | `Category — modifier — Brand` | `Running Shoes — Free Returns — Brand` |
| Homepage | `Brand — what you do` | `WebXDevelop — Free SEO Analyzer` |

-   Use one consistent separator across the site — `–`, `—`, or `|`.
-   Include the brand on most pages for recognition; drop it on space-tight pages where the topic needs every pixel.
-   Write for a human scanning ten blue links. Specificity and clarity win the click.

## Title tag vs H1

They serve different audiences — the search result versus the page itself — and they do not have to match exactly. See [how many H1 tags a page should have](/blog/how-many-h1-tags) for the full comparison. Keep them **close in meaning**: a large mismatch, where the title promises one thing and the H1 and content deliver another, reads as a bait-and-switch to both users and Google.

## Why Google rewrites titles

Since 2021, Google has rewritten the displayed title for a significant share of results. It tends to replace titles that are:

-   **too long** and get truncated mid-thought
-   **keyword-stuffed** or repetitive — `Plumber London | Plumber London | Best Plumber London`
-   **boilerplate** — every page titled just "Home" or the site name
-   **missing** entirely, in which case Google falls back to your H1 or the anchor text pointing at the page

The fix is to write a clear, appropriately sized, descriptive title in the first place. Google keeps well-written titles most of the time, and when it does substitute, it usually pulls the replacement from your **H1** — so keep that strong too.

## Uniqueness

Every indexable page needs a **distinct** title. Duplicate titles across pages are one of the most common audit findings: they confuse users in the search results and make it harder for Google to tell your pages apart. Templated titles are fine as long as the variable part makes each one genuinely unique.

## Common mistakes

-   Leading with the brand name on every page.
-   Keyword stuffing or repeating the primary term.
-   Writing a title that does not match the page's actual content or the intent it targets.
-   Rewriting titles every week chasing rankings — pick a good one and let it settle.
-   Letting the CMS output "Untitled" or the bare site name across the whole site.
-   Ignoring how the title looks truncated on a phone.
